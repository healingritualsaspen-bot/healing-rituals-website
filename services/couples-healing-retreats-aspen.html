The approach

You do not have to experience it the same way

A shared wellness experience can begin with a simple wish: to spend time together without organizing the household, solving a problem or looking at a phone. The purpose is not to produce identical emotions or force a conversation. It is to make room for attention.

Each partner brings a different inner world. Separate intention setting and individual guided work can honor that difference before shared sound, gentle breath or reflection. You can be close without giving up privacy, and you can choose different levels of participation.

The everyday application is often modest and important: noticing when you are becoming defensive, asking a clearer question or giving your partner time to finish. A shared session is an opportunity to consider those habits, not an obligation to forgive, agree or disclose.

Lauren’s couples offering is a wellness experience, not licensed couples psychotherapy. It can sit alongside work with your therapist, while focusing on rest, personal intention and a meaningful period of connection. Both people’s willingness matters throughout.

Planning your time

A session that fits your life

Couples visiting Aspen from elsewhere in Colorado or overseas can plan a shared experience around a stay in the valley. Local partners are equally welcome; reconnection does not require a long trip or a special anniversary.

A common question

Must we share everything from individual sessions?

No. Decide what feels appropriate to share. A private insight can remain private, and no one should be pressured to reveal personal material or take part in a practice to satisfy their partner.
