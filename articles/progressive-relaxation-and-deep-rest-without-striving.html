Letting effort soften: what progressive relaxation teaches about deep rest

Use the logic of progressive relaxation to make guided deep rest more comfortable—without forcing the body to relax.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 25, 2026

In this article

Rest can include tension

Deep rest does not require every muscle to release. Sometimes the body settles unevenly: the jaw softens while the shoulders stay guarded, or the breath becomes quieter while thoughts continue. Treating tension as failure usually adds another layer of effort.

What one comparison study measured

Toussaint and colleagues randomized 60 healthy undergraduates to progressive muscle relaxation, deep breathing, guided imagery or a control condition. Each active exercise increased reported immediate relaxation compared with control, while physiological patterns differed. Read the study. This was a brief laboratory comparison, not evidence of lasting sleep or mental-health outcomes.

Borrow the principle, not a rigid script

You can borrow a simple principle from progressive relaxation: noticing contrast. Feel the weight of one supported area, then a less-settled area, without demanding that either change. Let the guide’s voice be an invitation rather than an instruction you must execute. Adjust position, open your eyes or stop whenever comfort asks for it.

Finish with orientation

At the end, allow time to notice the room, move slowly and decide what comes next. If lying still increases pain, panic, traumatic recall or dissociation, use shorter, eyes-open practice or seek clinical guidance. Explore daytime rest, guided imagery research, or Guided Deep Rest.
