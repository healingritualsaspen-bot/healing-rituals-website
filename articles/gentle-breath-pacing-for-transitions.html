Everyday practice

A gentle breath-pacing practice for life’s transitions

The moments between roles are easy to rush through. A brief, comfortable breathing practice can mark the transition without asking you to perform or push.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 16, 2026

Use breath as a threshold

Try this when you close a laptop, park the car or arrive home. Let the first few breaths stay natural. Then allow the exhale to become slightly longer than the inhale only if that feels easy. Continue for a minute or two. The purpose is not to maximize lung capacity, hold your breath or create intense sensation. It is to notice that one part of the day has ended before the next begins.

What one randomized study found

In a 2023 randomized controlled study, 108 adults were assigned to five minutes a day of one of three structured breathing practices or mindfulness meditation for 28 days. All groups improved in positive affect and reduced anxiety; the breathing groups showed greater improvement in positive affect, with exhale-focused cyclic sighing performing best on some measures. The intervention was brief, remote and studied in generally healthy adults, so it does not establish that every breathwork style treats anxiety. Read the study.

Comfort is the governing rule

If lengthening the exhale creates air hunger, dizziness, tingling or distress, return to normal breathing. Keep your eyes open if that feels steadier. People with respiratory, cardiovascular, neurological or pregnancy-related concerns should discuss breath practices with an appropriate clinician. Gentle breathwork should not involve prolonged holds, rapid over-breathing or pressure to continue through discomfort.

Attach it to something already real

Consistency is easier when the cue already exists: after locking the office door, before entering the house, or once the kettle is on. Ask, “What am I carrying forward, and what can stay here?” The breath creates a small interval in which the answer can become visible. Read gentle breathwork between busy moments, review the slow-breathing evidence, or explore Gentle Breathwork.
