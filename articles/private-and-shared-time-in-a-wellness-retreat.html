Private time and shared time: finding the right retreat rhythm

How to balance guided experiences, companionship and genuine solitude without making a retreat feel isolating or crowded.

From Healing Rituals · Published October 9, 2026

Privacy and solitude are not the same

Privacy can mean freedom from ordinary roles; solitude means being physically or socially alone. One may feel spacious while the other feels difficult. A private retreat can include both guided companionship and protected quiet.

What retreat studies cannot isolate

A systematic review reported changes across varied residential retreat programs, but bundled activities and study limitations made it impossible to identify which components caused outcomes. Read the review. Research does not establish an ideal retreat schedule.

Plan three kinds of time

Plan guided time, unstructured nearby support and genuinely private time. Clarify whether the guide is reachable during breaks and how plans can change.

Let preference change during the retreat

Someone traveling to Aspen may want more recovery after altitude and travel; someone local may prefer a shorter container. Neither needs to maximize solitude. Explore retreat spacing, re-entry, or private retreats.
