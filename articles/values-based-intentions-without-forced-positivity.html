Intention guide

Choosing an intention that feels true—not artificially positive

How values reflection can shape a believable intention without requiring you to deny grief, anger or uncertainty.

An intention does not have to sound radiant

An intention can be honest and unfinished. “I am completely fearless” may create inner resistance when fear is present. “I want to meet this conversation with courage and care” leaves room for reality while naming a direction.

What self-affirmation research studied

In self-affirmation research, participants typically reflected on personally important values before receiving health information. A meta-analysis found small positive effects on message acceptance, intentions and behavior. Read the review. This is different from repeating a positive statement or completing a personalized guided journey.

Begin with a value, then choose a direction

Name one value—honesty, tenderness, freedom, steadiness or belonging. Ask what that value would look like in one recognizable situation. An intention such as “I can pause long enough to answer honestly” is easier to connect with action than a sweeping promise about who you must become.

Let the language become more believable

Read the intention aloud. If your body meets it with a hard no, soften it: “I am willing to practice…” or “I am making room for…” Language can evolve as experience changes. Continue with if–then planning, imagery and choice, or the signature approach.
