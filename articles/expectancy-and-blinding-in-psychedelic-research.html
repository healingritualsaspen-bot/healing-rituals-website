Research literacy

Why expectancy makes psychedelic research unusually difficult to interpret

How obvious subjective effects can unblind a clinical trial—and why promising findings still require careful reading.

From Healing Rituals · Published October 2, 2026

The experience can reveal the assignment

In a conventional blinded trial, participants and researchers should not know who received the active treatment. Psychedelic effects are often noticeable, making it easier to guess the assignment. That guess can shape hope, reporting, therapist behavior and interpretation.

Expectancy is not the same as “imaginary”

Muthukumaraswamy and colleagues reviewed expectancy and blinding problems in psychedelic randomized trials. They argued that treatment effects may be overestimated when participants become unblinded and arrive with strong expectations. Read the review. This does not prove that outcomes are only expectancy effects; it identifies uncertainty in how much change should be attributed to the drug and treatment package.

What reviewers found missing

The authors found that many trials did not adequately measure pretreatment expectancy or whether blinding succeeded. Small, highly selected samples and intensive support further limit generalization. A dramatic headline rarely communicates those design questions.

Read promising results with both openness and rigor

Preparation can acknowledge hope without promising a result. Integration can hold powerful experiences without treating every insight as literal truth. Healing Rituals does not provide dosing advice or substitute for licensed clinical care. Explore set and setting, integration, or scope and support.

Ask better questions when reading a headline

Look for the number of participants, diagnosis and screening criteria; what psychotherapy or preparation accompanied the drug; how the control condition worked; whether participants guessed their assignment; when outcomes were measured; and how adverse events were reported. Also ask whether the article describes an immediate experience or durable clinical change. These questions do not dismiss the research. They help preserve what is genuinely known while keeping hope from becoming certainty.
