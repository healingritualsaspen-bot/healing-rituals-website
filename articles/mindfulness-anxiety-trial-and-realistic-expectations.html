Mindfulness and anxiety: what a comparison with medication actually showed

Read a major anxiety trial carefully, including what MBSR involved, what the comparison means and why wellness sessions are different.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 25, 2026

A headline can hide the intervention

A study headline may make “mindfulness” sound like a few calming breaths. In this trial it meant an eight-week manualized Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction program with trained instructors, weekly classes, a retreat day and home practice. The medication group received escitalopram with clinical monitoring.

What the trial compared

Among 276 adults with diagnosed anxiety disorders, anxiety scores improved in both groups, and MBSR met the study’s predefined noninferiority margin at eight weeks. Adverse events were reported more often in the medication group. Read the trial. Noninferiority is a specific statistical conclusion; it does not mean the interventions are interchangeable for each person.

Why the result does not settle your choice

Treatment choice involves symptom severity, preference, access, medical history, time, cost and prior response. Medication should never be started, stopped or changed based on a wellness article. A brief guided-rest or breath session is not MBSR and cannot inherit this evidence.

Borrow a principle, not the treatment claim

The practical lesson is smaller: skills can require structure, repetition and support. If inward focus heightens anxiety, begin with eyes open, external sensory anchors or professional guidance. Seek urgent help for suicidality, mania, psychosis or severe functional decline. Explore grounding, relaxation limits, or the anxiety topic.

Verified source
