What research says about Reiki and gentle bodywork

Energy work is not a single treatment, so it does not have a single evidence base. Reiki, polarity therapy and craniosacral therapy need to be considered separately.

Reiki and anxiety

A 2024 meta-analysis brought together 13 studies involving 824 adults and reported an overall reduction in anxiety with Reiki. Results varied across populations and settings; benefits for preoperative and death-related anxiety were less consistent. The finding supports further investigation of anxiety outcomes, rather than establishing a mechanism of energy transfer. Read Guo and colleagues’ analysis.

A useful next question is what Reiki was compared with. Quiet attention, touch and expectations can contribute to how a session feels, and strong research tries to account for those factors.

Craniosacral research has mixed findings

A randomized sham-controlled neck-pain trial reported benefits from craniosacral therapy. Read Haller and colleagues’ trial. A broader 2024 review, however, found no clinically relevant benefit for the conditions it assessed. Read the later review.

Both belong in an honest overview. A positive individual study should not be presented as the final answer when subsequent synthesis reaches a different conclusion. Nor can a study of clinical treatment validate every gentler, craniosacral-inspired wellness practice.

Where polarity fits

Polarity therapy has also been studied, including a randomized trial comparing it with enhanced respite for family dementia caregivers. This is a narrow research context, not a basis for general claims about disease treatment or spiritual activation. See the Korn and colleagues trial.

The practical distinction is between exploring a tradition for personal well-being and presenting it as an established medical intervention. You can value an experience while asking careful questions about what a particular study shows.

What this means when choosing a session

For a Reiki or energy-work inquiry in the Roaring Fork Valley, ask about training, touch, comfort and the purpose of the session. If you are seeking help for ongoing pain or significant mental-health symptoms, include your treating professional in that discussion. Choose the service for what it actually offers, not a promise attached to a broad label.
