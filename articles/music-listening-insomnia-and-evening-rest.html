Music and sleep: a softer evening ritual, not a guaranteed remedy

What a Cochrane review found about listening to music for adult insomnia—and how to create a low-pressure evening ritual.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 25, 2026

In this article

A ritual can help without becoming a test

When sleep feels fragile, even a pleasant ritual can become another performance: choose the perfect track, relax quickly, fall asleep on cue. A gentler use of music begins earlier. Let it mark the transition from doing to resting, without making sleep the immediate assignment.

What the insomnia review found

A 2022 Cochrane review included 13 studies and 1,007 adults with insomnia symptoms. Listening to music may improve self-reported sleep quality compared with no treatment or usual care, but evidence for insomnia severity and objective measures was limited or uncertain. Read the review. The studies examined music listening, not singing bowls, live sound journeys or Healing Rituals sessions.

Design for comfort, not optimization

Choose sound that feels predictable, comfortable and easy to stop. Keep the volume low, avoid headphones if they are uncomfortable in bed, and notice whether rhythm or lyrics keep you alert. You might listen during a short transition before bed rather than all night. If silence is more settling, silence is a valid choice.

Know when sleep needs clinical care

Persistent insomnia can be connected with medical conditions, medications, mood and sleep disorders. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia is a first-line clinical treatment; a wellness ritual is not a substitute. Explore sensory comfort, sleep research, or sound sessions.
