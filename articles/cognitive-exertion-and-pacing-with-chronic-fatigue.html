Thinking takes energy too: pacing cognitive exertion with chronic fatigue

How to account for reading, screens, decisions and conversation when post-exertional malaise is not caused by movement alone.

From Healing Rituals · Published October 9, 2026

In this article

Exertion is broader than exercise

Reading, planning, conversation, screens and emotional concentration can all require energy. When worsening is delayed, the cognitive cost may be easy to miss.

What energy management includes

NICE energy-management guidance includes cognitive, physical, emotional and social activity and advises staying within current energy limits rather than following fixed increases. Read the guidance.

Break up decisions and sensory demand

Reduce simultaneous demands: lower screen brightness, pause between decisions, shorten forms and schedule quiet after appointments. Track delayed effects without creating a quota.

Do not turn pacing into mental avoidance

Pacing protects capacity; it is not a claim that thinking causes the illness. Persistent fatigue needs medical assessment, and post-exertional malaise should not be pushed through. Continue with rest before the crash, energy diaries, or chronic-fatigue support.
