Set, setting and support: what psychedelic preparation can—and cannot—control

A careful look at set and setting in psychedelic research, with practical questions for legal, supported preparation and integration.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 25, 2026

Context matters, but it is not control

“Set and setting” refers broadly to the person’s expectations and state, and the physical and social context around an experience. Preparation can reduce avoidable uncertainty, but it cannot guarantee a pleasant experience or a therapeutic outcome. Psychedelic experiences can be destabilizing as well as meaningful.

What the pooled research examined

Studerus and colleagues pooled data from controlled psilocybin studies in healthy volunteers. Features including emotional excitability, recent psychological distress, dose and environmental support were associated with aspects of acute response. Read the study. Participants were screened and monitored in research settings; the findings do not validate unsupervised use or Lauren’s wellness services.

Preparation means making room for uncertainty

Responsible preparation includes legal context, medical and psychiatric screening by qualified professionals, clarity about who provides care, an emergency plan and freedom to decline. It does not include finding a perfect intention or suppressing fear. Healing Rituals does not provide illegal substances, dosing advice or substitute for licensed psychedelic-assisted treatment.

Integration protects against over-interpretation

Afterward, allow meaning to develop slowly. A vivid insight is not automatically a fact or instruction. Avoid major irreversible decisions while unsettled, and seek urgent help for suicidality, mania, psychosis, severe insomnia or inability to function. Read questions before and after, integration beyond interpretation, or the scope of support.
