Why an inner image can feel emotionally real—and how to use that carefully

Explore what mental-imagery research suggests about emotion, then turn a meaningful image into one grounded everyday choice.

From Healing Rituals · Published September 25, 2026

Images can carry emotional weight

An inner image can sometimes arrive with more emotional force than a sentence. It may gather sensation, memory and possibility into one scene: a doorway, a steady flame, a future conversation handled differently. That vividness can be useful, but it is not proof that an image predicts the future or reveals a hidden biological truth. It is an experience worth meeting with curiosity.

What researchers actually reviewed

Holmes and Mathews reviewed laboratory and clinical evidence on mental imagery and emotion. Their paper describes imagery as especially capable of influencing emotion compared with verbal processing in some experimental settings, while also noting its role in distressing memories and worry. Read the review. The literature spans different tasks and populations. It does not show that a personalized guided journey automatically produces lasting change.

Give the image a job in ordinary life

After a session, choose one image that felt supportive and connect it to a recognizable moment. A shoreline might become a reminder to pause before agreeing to another commitment. A spacious room might cue one honest sentence in a difficult conversation. The value is not in repeating the image perfectly; it is in letting it support a small action you can observe.

Keep meaning flexible

Images can also feel uncomfortable or become less meaningful over time. You can revise, replace or release them. If imagery intensifies traumatic memories, panic or dissociation, stop and seek appropriately trained clinical support. Continue with real-life cues, self-critical patterns, or the signature approach.
