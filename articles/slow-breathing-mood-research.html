Slow breathing, stress and mood: what the evidence suggests

Breathwork includes very different practices. Evidence about a comfortable, slower rhythm should not be used to make the same claims for every intensive breathing method.

From Healing Rituals · 2 min read · Updated September 16, 2026

In this article

What a few minutes a day can tell us

In a 2023 randomized study, participants practiced brief breathing exercises or mindfulness meditation over a month. The breathwork conditions, especially exhale-focused cyclic sighing, showed improvements in positive mood and reduced respiratory rate relative to mindfulness. Read Balban and colleagues’ study.

This was a structured comparison of defined practices. It does not establish that every breathing exercise produces the same result, or that a stronger sensation means a greater benefit.

What happens with repeated slow breathing

A separate 12-week study found reductions in psychological stress with regular slow breathing. It compared different inhalation-to-exhalation ratios, reminding us that the rhythm and the practice schedule are part of the intervention. Read Birdee and colleagues’ trial.

The relevant idea for everyday life is repetition that remains comfortable. A practice people can actually use may matter more than a technique that sounds impressive but is difficult to sustain.

The broader research picture

A 2023 meta-analysis of randomized trials found small-to-medium benefits for self-reported stress and related mental-health outcomes. The authors also highlighted study limitations and the need for more rigorous research. Read the analysis.

This gives a reasonable basis for exploring breathwork as a supportive practice, not a promise that it treats every cause of anxiety or substitutes for needed care.

Applying the evidence without overcomplicating it

Ask which breathing method will be used, how it will be adapted and what to do if it feels unpleasant. You should be able to return to ordinary breathing without being encouraged to push through.

Someone looking for gentle breathwork in Colorado may want a practical skill for daily stress, while an Aspen retreat visitor may prefer a short transition into a sound session. Both can begin with comfort and a clear purpose rather than intensity.
